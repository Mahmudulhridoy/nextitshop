"Phone verified" sounds simple, but it covers very different things. The type of number used to verify an account affects how long it stays active and how often it is asked to prove itself again.

## The three common types

### Real SIM numbers

These are issued by mobile carriers and tied to a physical SIM card. Platforms generally treat them as the most trustworthy because they look like ordinary customers.

- **Pros:** Highest trust, fewer extra checks
- **Cons:** Higher cost, limited supply

### VoIP numbers

These run over the internet and are provided by calling apps and services. Many platforms can recognise the number ranges VoIP providers use.

- **Pros:** Cheap and quick to get
- **Cons:** More likely to be challenged or restricted

### Temporary virtual numbers

Shared, short-lived numbers used to receive a single code. Because many people use them, a single number may already have been used on the platform before.

- **Pros:** Very low cost
- **Cons:** Lowest reliability, often rejected outright

## Why it matters

A platform's trust in an account often begins with the number behind it. A trusted number means fewer verification prompts, while a weak one can mean repeated challenges or restrictions soon after sign-up.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does PVA stand for?

PVA means phone verified account, an account confirmed with a mobile number.

### Is a real SIM better than VoIP for verification?

Real SIM numbers are usually trusted more by platforms than VoIP numbers, which are easier to detect.

### What should I ask a seller about verification?

Ask what type of number was used, whether you can change recovery details and what the replacement policy covers.
