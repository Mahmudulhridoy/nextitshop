## What You Receive

Accounts are delivered in a Username : Pass : 2FA : Recovery format. That includes the 2FA details and recovery information so you can secure the account immediately. The accounts are ready for API access and for **GitHub Copilot**, subject to GitHub's own plans and eligibility.

## Who Uses Extra GitHub Accounts?

- **Agencies and freelancers** keeping client work separate
- **Teams** that need a shared automation or service account
- **Open-source maintainers** with a separate project identity
- **QA and DevOps engineers** testing CI pipelines, tokens and permissions
- **Learners** wanting a clean account for tutorials

A contribution graph shows when activity happened, but employers and clients care far more about the quality of your repositories than the number of green squares.

## What to Check Before You Buy

1. 2FA and recovery information are included
2. You can change the email and password
3. The account has no existing restrictions
4. A replacement policy is available

## Set It Up Professionally

1. Change the password and add your own email
2. Keep 2FA enabled and store recovery codes offline
3. Add an SSH key and review authorised apps
4. Create a profile README and pin your best repositories
5. Enable branch protection on important repositories

Our guide to [GitHub profile and security](/blog/github-account-profile-and-security) shows each step in detail.

## Keep Your Account in Good Standing

Follow GitHub's Terms of Service and Acceptable Use Policies. Avoid automated bulk activity such as mass starring or following, and do not commit secrets or tokens to public repositories.
