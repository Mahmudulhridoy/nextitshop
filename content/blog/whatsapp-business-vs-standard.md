Plenty of small businesses still answer customers from a personal WhatsApp. It works until the volume grows, a message gets lost, or a staff member leaves with the phone. Here is how the two apps differ and when it makes sense to switch.

## What WhatsApp Business adds

**A real business profile.** Your name, address, opening hours, website and description are visible to anyone who opens the chat. Customers see a business, not a random number.

**Quick replies.** Save answers to questions you get daily, such as prices, delivery times and payment details, and send them with a shortcut like `/price`.

**Greeting and away messages.** Welcome new customers automatically, and let people know when you will reply outside working hours.

**Labels.** Tag chats as "New", "Awaiting payment" or "Delivered" so nobody has to scroll to find out where an order stands.

**Catalog.** List products with photos and prices inside the app, so customers can browse without leaving the chat.

## When the standard app is enough

- You only chat with friends and family
- You rarely receive customer enquiries
- You do not need a public profile

## When to move to Business

- You answer the same questions every day
- More than one person helps with replies
- You want customers to find your hours and catalog easily

## Setting it up properly

1. Use a number that is **dedicated to the business**, not your personal one.
2. Fill in every profile field. Complete profiles look more trustworthy.
3. Write three to five quick replies for your most common questions.
4. Create labels that match your real order flow.
5. Back up chats regularly.

## A note on good practice

WhatsApp expects businesses to message people who have agreed to hear from them. Keep your contact lists opt-in. It protects your number and your reputation.
