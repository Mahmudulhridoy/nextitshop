If you are looking to **buy Gmail accounts**, the first decision is simple: do you need a fresh account or an aged one? The answer depends on what you plan to do with it. This guide explains the differences between fresh and aged **Gmail PVA accounts** (phone verified accounts), what each is best for, and the checks to run before and after delivery.

## What Is a Gmail PVA Account?

A Gmail PVA account is a Google account that was verified with a phone number during sign-up. Phone verification links the account to a real number, which helps Google treat the account as an ordinary user rather than an anonymous registration. A good PVA account also includes recovery details, so you can regain access if something goes wrong.

## Fresh vs Aged Gmail Accounts

| Type | Best for | Things to know |
|------|----------|----------------|
| Fresh PVA | Testing, one-off sign-ups, backup inboxes | Newest option and the most affordable. Build usage gradually. |
| Aged 1+ year | Business inboxes, tools that review account history | Longer history, established usage pattern |
| Aged 5+ years | Long-term use where account history matters | The most established option, limited availability |

Age by itself is not a guarantee of anything. What matters is that the account was maintained normally and that recovery details are in order.

## What to Check Before You Buy

1. **Delivery format.** You should receive the email, password and recovery details in a clear format.
2. **Recovery information.** Confirm you can update the recovery phone and email once the account is yours.
3. **IMAP and POP3 access.** If you plan to use an email client, make sure these are enabled.
4. **Replacement policy.** A written policy for sign-in problems is a sign of a serious seller.
5. **Support.** You should be able to reach a real person quickly if something is wrong at delivery.
